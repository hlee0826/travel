# Integrated Garden Reference Library v2

This is an experimental, additive method for generating Korean minhwa pet-portrait composition references. It does not replace the commissioned portrait workflow, identity study, approval gates, or `GUIDELINE.md`.

## Purpose

The library tests two qualities observed in the supplied contemporary references:

1. the pet reads as an individual through a small, explicit set of anatomical and marking anchors while remaining visibly painted; and
2. the pet and garden behave as one composition through shared pigment, edge rhythm, weight balance, and either a believable fauna interaction or a calm direct-to-viewer portrait relationship.

The plates use fictional pets. They are composition and rendering references, not claims of likeness to real animals. A customer commission must still begin with intake, a plain-hanji identity study, the four likeness tests, and customer approval.

## Method: identity lock + garden envelope + interaction vector

### 1. Identity lock

Give each fictional subject five to eight concrete anchors: skull width, eye spacing and color, ear carriage, muzzle ratio, marking boundaries, body proportion, tail, coat direction, and one characteristic expression. Put these before style language in every prompt. Breed names are optional shorthand only.

### 2. Pose skeleton

Define the weight-bearing structure before decoration: which paws touch the ground, the direction of the spine, the turn of the head, the tail path, and whether the silhouette is compact, vertical, horizontal, or diagonal. Protect clear space around the ears, muzzle, paws, and tail.

### 3. Garden envelope

Use one major motif mass entering from one edge and one smaller continuation or grounding mass from a different edge. The two masses form an asymmetric envelope around the pose rather than a wreath. Keep 28–36% quiet hanji and avoid a hard horizon.

### 4. Interaction vector or direct portrait

For interaction plates, one butterfly or magpie is the active counterpart. Align the pet's pupils, muzzle, or lifted paw toward it. For direct-portrait calibration plates, keep the pet's torso and head square to the viewer, maintain direct eye contact, and place the fauna outside the gaze line as environmental symbolism. A runtime set deliberately mixes both modes so curiosity remains available without becoming the default pose.

### 5. Material bridge

Paint fur, petals, leaves, water, bark, and rock with the same family of warm ink contours, translucent mineral/plant-like pigment, controlled barim, and visible hanji. The face has the highest detail; the torso is simplified; the garden is one step quieter.

### 6. Text exclusion

These references contain no pet name, Korean or Latin lettering, seal, watermark, frame, caption, or reserved inscription column. The negative space belongs to the composition.

## Garden recipes

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peony & butterflies | One irregular peony branch, 15–24% frame | Small rock or low peony continuation | 2–3 unequal butterflies | warm hanji, jade, shell white, coral/peony red, mineral blue |

## Set architecture

The original 20-plate core gives each species ten plates: three peony, three lotus, two plum, and two pine. Fifteen exploratory extensions broadened the garden and pose pool. Six direct-to-viewer calibration plates—one cat and one dog per customer-facing garden—bring the current source library to 41 images. Every plate changes pose, crop, balance, interaction, and fictional identity while keeping the medium and hierarchy invariant. See `manifest.yaml` and `prompts.md` for the documented core and calibration additions.

## Commission use

Choose a plate by pose skeleton and garden envelope, not by superficial coat resemblance. Replace the fictional identity lock with the approved customer's recognition anchors. Generate the plain identity study first, pass all four likeness tests, then transfer the approved identity into the selected recipe. A final integrated artwork must pass both gates and score at least 82/100.
