# Prompt record

These are the normalized final prompts for the accepted reference set. Combine the shared base with one plate module. The accepted image filenames are listed in `image-index.md`.

## Shared base

```text
Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset type: project reference plate for a Korean minhwa pet-portrait workflow.

Create one finished standalone vertical 4:5 Korean minhwa painting. The fictional pet must read as an individual through the stated anchors, not as a generic breed. Use Korean minhwa visual language generalized from historical flower-and-animal painting, not an imitation of any named living artist: fine warm-ink contours, translucent mineral and plant-like pigments, controlled barim, matte warm mulberry hanji with subtle fibers, shallow stacked symbolic space, affectionate asymmetry, and no photographic horizon. Pet, flora, rock, water, and small fauna share one brush-and-pigment material. Eyes and face hold peak detail; torso is simplified; garden is one step quieter. Preserve 28–36% quiet paper and clean space around ears, muzzle, whiskers, markings, paws, and tail.

One pet only; coherent anatomy and clearly readable weight-bearing paws. No generated text in any language, pet name, Korean lettering, seal, signature, watermark, border, frame, or app UI. No photographic cutout, anime eyes, glossy 3D, oil impasto, deep perspective, floral wreath, centered symmetry, hanbok, crown, palace, temple, random pan-Asian ornaments, malformed anatomy, or motif overlap on recognition anchors.
```

## Direct-to-viewer calibration modules

The following six additions share one corrective instruction: one fictional pet
sits naturally with its torso square to the viewer, head level, both eyes making
direct viewer contact, both front paws visible, and a relaxed tail. The
butterflies or magpie remain environmental symbols outside the pet's gaze line.
Avoid side profile, three-quarter head turn, upward gaze, or reaching toward the
fauna. Maintain the shared base medium, hierarchy, quiet space, and exclusions.
